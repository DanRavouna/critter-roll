## Wind: the world moves (Dan: "the world doesn't feel lively enough", built Nov 2026 from the plan he approved: 6 frames, lamp gust, drift later)
- Meaning per place (Dan: "wind symbolises change, but not in the cold, where it symbolises stillness"; then "not constant except in a windy place, a wind variable per map"): `WINDS` in world.js gives each region a character, `{ base, calm }` (calm = the share of time with no wind; 0 = it never stops): Sproutvale 0.5 / 0.5 (a breeze that comes and goes), Moss Forest and Mossbrook 0.55 / 0.6 (sheltered: soft breaths between long calms), Tide Shore, Coralbay and Reef 0.85 / 0.05 (sea wind, always moving), Highland Pass 0.8 / 0.1 (a pass), Ember Fields, Cinderport and Ashwood 1.0 / 0.25 (restless: hard gusts, short calms); frost, Frosthold, Glacier Coast, the Cold Ascent and Vigil Peak have base 0: NOTHING moves there, not even in a storm or at the lamp's gust. Caves, dungeons and interiors are 0. Reduce motion (`settings.calm` or the OS setting) is 0 everywhere. `windLevel(mapId, t, region)` (pure, exposed as `WORLD.windLevel`) turns the character into a strength 0..1 that changes over time from three slow waves (23, 37 and 61 s, phases seeded by the map id, base varied +-15% per map) so it never repeats in lockstep and two forest maps do not breathe together. Then in `windNow()`: storm x1.4 + 0.25, rain x1.15, fog x0.5, NIGHT x0.6 (calmer), a gust = 1. Strength picks the amplitude class (0 = still pose, 1 about 1 px at the crown, 2 double; thresholds with hysteresis) and the clock (`fps = 2.5 + 2.5 x strength`); a change of class crossfades over 0.3 s (the previous frame is drawn under the new one; that second blit exists only during the fade). Unknown regions default to base 0.5 / calm 0.4.
- How: `PXW.terrain` (pxworld.js) no longer paints trees, tall/rare grass, flowers and water highlights into the still image; it RECORDS them (`canvas.anim.items`, same seeded positions as before, so every map looks exactly as it did) and `PXW.bake(base, pose k 0..5, amplitude A 0..2)` paints a whole frame: the still image plus those things leaning with a sine pose (`SIN6`), the top of a tree moving most and the foot not at all, with a small lag down the stem so it bends; each tile has a phase that is a SMOOTH function of its position (`phaseAt`, no noise: one wave rolls west to east, wavelength about 12 tiles, tilted slightly; Dan: the animations should gradually offset).  Irregularity (Dan: not too perfect): about 25% of trees (12% of tufts and flowers) break from the wave by one pose and each tree has its own stiffness 0.7 to 1.15 (seeded by position, stable). (Amplitude classes are now about how many rows flutter, see below; nothing moves more than 1 px.) Frame 0 at amplitude 0 is the old still picture. Frames bake lazily (`PXW.fill`, one every 120 ms; frame 0 at load), so a map still opens as fast. World draws one blit of the current frame, cropped to the screen (cropping also made the draw cheaper than before). Clock: `fps = 2.5 + 2.5 x strength` over the 6 poses. Extras: sparkles on rare grass and in the Sproutvale fountain; water highlights drift.
- Calmer, edges only (Dan: "the animations move too much", "only the bits outside of the tree should move, not the whole line" = the pixel row of the sprite; "you're on your own from there"): `swayStamp` no longer shears whole rows. Every horizontal run of pixels keeps its core; only its two ends flutter by at most ONE pixel at tile scale (2 on screen), growing (the end pixel copied outward) or shrinking (pulled in), only in the upper ~62% of the sprite, never on trunk runs (`t`/`T` only), and a lone pixel (twig, flower head) shifts as a whole. `V6 = [0, 0, 1, 0, 0, -1]`, so a thing moves in only two of its six poses and a gust shows as a band passing. Class 1 (breeze) touches about half of the rows, class 2 most; a stiff tree (small gain) fewer. Measured on the four tree sprites: solid trees keep 76 to 91% of their pixels identical in every pose and differ from rest by 3 to 8% on average (thin dead trees and palms 14 to 25% at full strength), nothing strays more than 1 px. Clock slowed to `fps = 1.5 + 1.5 x strength` (was 2.5 + 2.5), water highlights drift 1 px (was up to 3).
- Rhythm bug (Dan: "when I get into a place the trees go really fast then slow down"): the pose was `floor(tAnim x fps)`; `fps` follows the wind strength, which changes all the time, and `tAnim` is large, so every small change of speed threw the pose several steps ahead (measured before the fix: jumps of more than one pose, 6 in Ember and 4 in Moss in 12 s). Now the pose is a running sum, `windPh += fps x dt` (`windT` tracks the last clock). Test: `windrhythmtest.py` records the pose number on every frame for 12 s after entering Ember, Highland Pass and Moss (and Highland again) and checks the steps per second never exceed the wind's own pace and there are no jumps. LESSON: my earlier checks of the wind were stills (screenshots, filmstrips) and numbers per frame set; nothing looked at motion OVER TIME, so the rhythm was never reviewed. Anything animated must now be checked over real time (frame index against clock), see the Tests line in CLAUDE.md. I still cannot watch video or judge feel: only measurable rhythm.
- The lamp: lighting the great lamp calls `WORLD.gust()` (3.5 s at full strength in places that have wind; the cold stays still). `WORLD.wind()` and `WORLD.terrainCanvas()` are test hooks.
- Perf (4x CPU throttle, dpr 3, `perf2` style loop in Moss): about the same per frame as before (33 / 18 / 36 ms before, 44 / 15 / 35 ms after, noisy); the first attempt used a transparent overlay and a second blit and cost about 25 ms more, so frames are now whole pictures. Memory: about 1 to 1.5 MB per frame, up to 6 frames per amplitude class in use.
- The weather takes its motion from the same wind (Dan: "if the snow falls slowly and straight there is no wind, you see?"): `WORLD` passes the map's current strength (`wind:`) to `WEATHER.draw`, which smooths it over 1.5 s (`ws`; `wf = min(1, 2 ws)` for flutter). Snow: fall speed x(0.55 + 0.45 wf), sideways drift `ws x (40 + 50 s)` px/s EAST plus a flutter that scales with `wf`: in still air (the whole cold) flakes fall slowly and dead straight, no wobble. Rain and storm now lean DOWN AND EAST (they used to lean west, against the trees): `slant = 0.03 + ws x 0.4` (0.55 in a storm). Ash drifts east with `ws`, fog speed `0.1 + 1.1 ws` of its old speed, pollen sways with `wf`. Reduce motion is unchanged (colour wash only). Test hooks `WEATHER._parts()` and `WEATHER._wind()`. Test: `winddrifttest.py` (cold snow 0 px/s sideways at about 26 px/s down; Highland Pass snow 53 px/s east and 64 in a gust; rain east and more in a storm, nearly straight in the cold; ash east). Not verified: fog (the test's fog check is vacuous: fog only moves sideways and the test counts falling particles), how it looks on a phone.
- Test: `windtest.py` (four minutes of each place's strength: calm share, strong share, calm spells; the cold has none; steady places never calm; storm does not move the cold; night is capped at 0.6; Reduce motion; the gust; six distinct frames at both amplitudes; nothing differs away from moving tiles; neighbours a step apart with a minority out of step; per-tree stiffness). Not verified: how it FEELS (tempo, whether 1 px sway reads as wind on a phone, whether the gust is noticeable), battery use, a long play session, low-end phones. Not built (plan phase 3): ambient drift particles (leaves, snow, embers, spray, chimney smoke), flags and banners.

## Architecture (single HTML, assembled from pieces in `world/`)
- `runtime.js` TPL: renders the screen markup (`sc-if`, `sc-for`, `dc-import`, `{{holes}}`) with DOM morphing; hole attributes renamed `data-h-*` at compile.
- `pixel.js` PIX: critter geometry → pixel sprites (`big` 0.25 scale, `icon` 0.075 shown 2x, `silhouette`), cached by seed:stage:mode.
- `pxworld.js` PXW: 16px pixel tiles per theme, buildings, player/NPC frames (`frames(pal)`).
- `world.js` WORLD: 5 maps (town 44x34 + 4 zones 34x28), movement, exits, locked-trail barricades, NPC sight lines, encounters, objective arrow, day/night tint, camera snapped to device pixels (no jitter).
- `cloud.js` CLOUD: cloud save to `data/users/{uid}/save`, lease (`acquire`, 4s) + re-check before every write, conflict dialog, local-first.
- `app.js`: `Base` → `Component` (game logic ported from the original canvas) → `Game` (overrides: make with levels, battleAction, party, trainers, encounters) → `APP` (HUD, menus, dialogue, people, map, backup, shop, party, bag, intro, hints, objective).
- `screen.html`: screen template (roll/dex/battle/trainer screens). Shell HTML holds HUD, overlays, CSS.
- Rebuild: `python3 build2.py` (JS order now runtime, pixel, pxworld, world, cloud, music, app) (self-contained: `world/shell.html` holds HUD/CSS/overlays with `/*@@JS@@*/` and `<!--@@SCREEN@@-->` placeholders; JS order runtime, pixel, pxworld, world, cloud, app). Output: /mnt/user-data/outputs/critter-roll-world.html. Buildings are drawn in `pxworld.js` `place()` (world.js `drawPlace` is dead code).

## Real-time battles (done as an opt-in beta, Nov 2026; Dan: "active combat, one button to attack, changeable attacks")

## Fix: the screen kept shaking after a duel (Nov 2026; Dan's phone report: "after defeating a trainer every entity started shaking uncontrollably")
- Cause: the camera shake (`VFX.shakeNow`, read by world.js every frame) was only decayed inside `VFX.step`, which `ACT.step` calls only while combat is running. In a city (no wild critters) ACT returns early after the duel ends, so the last blow's shake never faded. Now `shakeNow(t)` decays the shake by the clock it is read with, and `ACT.step` calls `VFX.clear()` whenever combat is not running (quiet zones, interiors). The same would have frozen hit-stop, which is also cleared. Regression: `vfxtest.py` shakes the camera in Mossbrook and checks it settles.

## Feedback round: scenery jitter + a level-design review (Nov 2026; Dan: "objects shake a lot when moving", "you follow my instructions but do not grasp the spirit, review it and fix what you find")

# Critter Roll: handoff (short core)

Read this first. It is deliberately short. The full per-phase history (every system, decision and "not verified" note) is in `docs/handoff-archive.md`: search it by section title when a task touches that system (for example "Action mode phase 5", "Adventure part 2", "Weather + abilities"). `CLAUDE.md` holds the standing rules.

## Meaning (read this first; Dan, Nov 2026)
Humans read in metaphor. The game is the greater meaning; the quest is inside it; each level is inside the quest. Dan confirmed this reading of the existing text (he did not write the theme himself, so refine it with him rather than treat it as fixed).
- **The game (theme): home is something someone keeps lit for you, and the journey ends when you become the one who keeps it. Dan's refinement: becoming the keeper means you no longer need someone to keep it for you.** The ending is independence, not inheritance: lighting the lamp is the moment you realise you can carry your own light. The keepers (Maren, Hale, Orlen, the hermit) held it for you until you could, so their "prove you can carry it" is letting go, not gatekeeping. The early scaffolding (Mom, the Lab, the gifted partner, Pip's tips, Mira's potions) is the being-looked-after that the game should gradually and visibly withdraw. Guard rails: being looked after is good, not weakness; carrying your own light is not being alone (home and its people stay intact at the end); withdrawn support must be felt, not look like a bug. The ninth seat (the one left out) now supports this and no longer carries it. You start looked after (Mom, the Lab, a gifted partner), cross a land full of keepers who tend dormant things for a successor, and end by lighting the lamp and taking the ninth seat, which is the one the world left out. Evidence already in the text: eight statues and an empty ninth seat with an unclaimed crown; Keeper Orlen "Eight were enough for the world, they said. The ninth was kept here, asleep"; Veilcrown (Dusk, Last Light) and Wickling (Guiding Light); Warden Maren, Forgemaster Hale and Keeper Orlen each keep something dark or cold until someone proves they can carry it; Old Marta "Four fires and one lamp, and the whole country knew where home was"; Joss: the lighthouse lamp points inland at the arch, "whoever built it wanted the door found"; and the unexplained route props (new rope, second cup, warm lantern, new stone, new ribbon, polished runners, wet skiff, empty chair) all say someone tends this for someone not yet arrived.
- **The rule, refined by Dan (Nov 2026): hint and play the metaphor, don't tell it.** Play means the mechanic itself carries the meaning, across the whole game including the daily loop; every element of a place (mechanic, layout, what is seen and hidden, colours, sound, words, pacing) conveys the same intent; the hint is the introduction, a first smaller encounter with the concept placed anywhere earlier on the road, so the play feels already met; nothing explains. The player's road below is the structure the experience is built on: define it before building.
- **The quest:** the four seals are four fires you are trusted to tend (not trophies); the three side places are the three things that must be kept (the seat: who is counted; the light: the way home; the fire: the work); the Peak is the long watch. Lighting the lamp is the answer to the question the game asks from the first minute.
- **The contradictions found in the first reading, and where they stand (Nov 2026):** the spine was written as conquest -> DONE (seals handed over as fires, Vigil Peak / The Long Watch / Fire-keeper / Watchkeeper); the Tower was a floor counter -> DONE as the long watch (lobby room, keeper lines; floors and rewards unchanged); the deep layer reachable only through side content -> DONE (keepers rest, lighthouse beams, Home's window, the arch glows); cities and zones meant only their element -> DONE (one signature place each, four gym trials, every house a statement, architecture per region); weather lines are type trivia -> OPEN (`flavor.js` still says "Volt critters love this weather"); the four clue-givers are plot devices -> OPEN (Edda, Dell, Joss, Neri still deliver clues as exposition; Joss and Brandt got one letting-go line each). Dan's first three days (2.5 h, level 6, two seals) were all on the conquest spine; he has not yet reported a play-through of the revamp.
- **DRAFT meaning of each place (refine with Dan):**
  - Sproutvale: where you are kept. The lit window; a bed made and a shelf kept full in the two cottages (Nov 2026; the Shrine is gone).
  - Moss Forest / Mossbrook: being tended while you grow (Elder Wren: "grow together"); first seal = first fire.
  - Frost Peaks / Frosthold: what is kept by being kept cold; waiting preserved.
  - Ember Fields / Cinderport: the fire that has to be fed; work; the forge below is where it began.
  - Tide Shore / Coralbay: what the sea gives back; return; the last fire, where the road home begins.
  - The routes: the roads between the fires, kept walkable by people you mostly never meet; every route holds one sign of someone tending.
  - The Sealed Arch / Lamp Tower / Forge Below: the seat, the light, the fire.
  - Vigil Peak and the Long Watch: the long watch, where the floors are nights kept, not rivals beaten (built: names, keeper lines, a lobby room; every floor is a night, the visitors have plain names, the weekly rules are weathers of the nights).
  - Emphasis question settled by Dan (see theme): independence, you no longer need it kept for you.
- Revamp order, all five steps done (Nov 2026): the rule and this section; the spine and seals reworded; a meaning-bearing place per route, zone and city (then the gyms as trials, then every house); the Tower decided; the deep layer on the main path. What remains is listed under "To do" at the end.

## Linking the road (Nov 2026, Dan: "link everything into a meaningful and cohesive experience")
One verb runs the whole road: keep a light. Each station below is its meaning sentence, written before building (the rule).
- **Title screen**: the introduction to everything: one lit window in the dark, kept by someone before you did anything; the game begins when you touch that light.
- **Home, the two chairs**: a seat has always been counted for you; the first, quiet meeting of "who is counted", long before the ninth seat.
- **Tide Shore, the returned things**: what the sea gives back is met on the shore itself, before Coral asks you to answer a call.
- **The roads after the beacon**: the lamps that lit themselves ahead of you no longer do; from the night you light the great lamp, a post lights when you pass it and stays lit, saved: the roads are kept by you now.
- **Home after the beacon**: the window is dark until you light it, each day; being the keeper is a small daily act, not a line.
- **The Lamp Tower**: the way home is lit by you: the chambers are dark, each gate opens when you have stayed beside that chamber's lamp until it takes, and the great lamp lights only from the lamps you kept below it.
- **The daily loop**: a day holds twenty rolls and forty sightings, and nothing carries over: the rolls chip is the day's lamp and its flame goes down as the day is spent; what you keep is what comes home with you, and the critter you keep sleeps in the basket at the Lab; letting go costs nothing; the Shrine gives without being asked.
- **Night versions of the place themes**: at night the world is quieter, and each place is still itself in the dark: a melody written for the night over the day's chords (fewer notes, lower, held), slower on a softer wave with no drums and one bass note a bar: the hint, every night, of the quiet before someone comes to a door (the Watch).
- **The Watch's lobby**: a track of its own, slow and held, few notes that do not resolve: a lamp that does not go out, waiting.
- **The opening** (Dan, Nov 2026: "most players lose interest after a minute, the initial direction is difficult to feel"): you begin at the door of the house whose window you just touched, with the road in front of you; the first thing to keep is within a few steps (a small patch of tall grass beside the path); the one open gate, west, has a lamp lit on it by day, the road someone keeps for you, and the three others are shut; Fern points at what you can see. Nothing explains.
- **Frost's verb, Deep Freeze**: stillness. Press it and stand still: a ring of ice forms around you and widens the longer you stay; what it touches is held, slowed and chilled; the first step shatters it. The same verb Glacia's trial taught.
- **Ember's verb, Scorch**: the fire that has to be fed. Hold the attack: the flame at your critter's mouth grows while you feed it; let go and it goes out as a cone that burns. A tap is a puff.
- **Tide's verb, Undertow**: what comes back. A wave goes out ahead of you and comes back with whatever it touched, dragging it to your feet, soaked and weakened.
- **Moss's verb, Root Bind**: tending. Roots take hold of the nearest critter where it stands; it cannot step while they hold, it is drained, and while you stay beside it the roots feed you back.
- **Volt's verb, Arc Dash**: the wind that passes through. Your critter becomes a line of light for a moment and crosses three tiles through whatever stands there, leaving it dazed.
- **Dusk's verb, Veil**: the dark you step into. Your critter fades; what hunts it loses it, and the first blow out of the dark lands hard and ends the veil.
- **Stone's verb, Bulwark**: what is kept. You set a wall of stone where you face; it takes the blows and the charges meant for you until it has taken enough, and nothing passes it.
- **Bloom's verb, Soothing Scent**: gives without being asked. Petals bloom around you; you and yours heal while you stand in them, and what comes near forgets the fight for a moment.
- **The heavy blow, every type**: fed, not rolled. Hold the attack and the blow gathers; let go and it lands, always, as hard as you fed it. The 75% miss is gone: in a real-time game a dropped input is not a mechanic.
- **Guard, every type**: a stance, not a tap. Standing firm is something you hold, and you cannot hold it forever.
- **The first critter**: at a new game one wild critter waits in the grass by your door, so the first thing kept is at home, not found by luck.
- **The Watch's nights**: a night is kept by lasting it, not by beating whoever comes: the visitor comes in at the lobby door, the night runs its length (about half a minute, longer on the long nights) and ends at dawn with the lamp still lit, or earlier if they all go down; the lobby itself is where it happens, by the lamps that do not go out.

## Music and sound against the road (audited Nov 2026)
- The title is silent: audio unlocks on the first gesture, which is the touch of the light, so the first sound of the game is one you caused. Sproutvale's theme begins under Fern.

Ember drives: `ember` 126, `cinderport` 116, square wave, drums every step; the Descent uses `ember`. Moss and Mossbrook are slow triangle (92, 88). Tide and Coralbay 110 and 100 with a thin square. The Sealed Arch is 70 bpm with one hat per bar (the quiet of the seat); the Lamp Tower 88 triangle; the Forge 78.
- Changed: a night of the Watch played the boss track every time; now only the long nights (5, 10, 15, 20, `warden`) do, the others are battles. Highland Pass shares Vigil Peak's theme (`route-mf` -> `peak`): the first road and the last place sound alike, which fits the road's end being its beginning, kept.
- Built (Nov 2026): every outdoor place theme has a night version (`<id>@night`, built in `music.js` from `NIGHT_LEAD`: a melody written for the night over the day's chords, bpm x 0.78, triangle wave, no drums, one bass note a bar, volume 0.62), chosen by `musicTrack` when `timeOfDay()` is night outdoors; the Watch's lobby (`in-tower`) plays `watch` (72 bpm, triangle, held notes, one hat a bar). `musictest` pins both and renders their loudness. Tempo and feel cannot be judged from here.

## Architecture (single HTML, assembled from pieces in `world/`)
- `runtime.js` TPL: renders the screen markup (`sc-if`, `sc-for`, `dc-import`, `{{holes}}`) with DOM morphing; hole attributes renamed `data-h-*` at compile.
- `pixel.js` PIX: critter geometry → pixel sprites (`big` 0.25 scale, `icon` 0.075 shown 2x, `silhouette`), cached by seed:stage:mode.
- `pxworld.js` PXW: 16px pixel tiles per theme, buildings, player/NPC frames (`frames(pal)`).
- `world.js` WORLD: 5 maps (town 44x34 + 4 zones 34x28), movement, exits, locked-trail barricades, NPC sight lines, encounters, objective arrow, day/night tint, camera snapped to device pixels (no jitter).
- `cloud.js` CLOUD: cloud save to `data/users/{uid}/save`, lease (`acquire`, 4s) + re-check before every write, conflict dialog, local-first.
- `app.js`: `Base` → `Component` (game logic ported from the original canvas) → `Game` (overrides: make with levels, battleAction, party, trainers, encounters) → `APP` (HUD, menus, dialogue, people, map, backup, shop, party, bag, intro, hints, objective).
- `screen.html`: screen template (roll/dex/battle/trainer screens). Shell HTML holds HUD, overlays, CSS.
- Rebuild: `python3 build2.py` (JS order now runtime, pixel, pxworld, world, cloud, music, app) (self-contained: `world/shell.html` holds HUD/CSS/overlays with `/*@@JS@@*/` and `<!--@@SCREEN@@-->` placeholders; JS order runtime, pixel, pxworld, world, cloud, app). Output: /mnt/user-data/outputs/critter-roll-world.html. Buildings are drawn in `pxworld.js` `place()` (world.js `drawPlace` is dead code).

## The engine is its own folder (Nov 2026, milestone 1 of `docs/next-plan.md`)

## Tests: how the process works now (Nov 2026)
- Setup once: `bash src/test-setup.sh`. Run: `bash src/run-tests.sh @smoke` after EVERY change (about 30 s); the tests of the feature you touched (`bash src/run-tests.sh name.py`); the whole suite once before pushing a batch (no argument, 78 tests, about 4.5 minutes with 6 side by side; the suite is mostly fixed sleeps, about 480 s of `wait_for_timeout` in 1100 s of test time, so the CPU stays half idle even at 6 jobs; `JOBS=3` is the quiet setting for diagnosing a flaky test, and the real-time wind tests held up under load). Do not add the groups after a full run, they are already in it.
- The runner prints one line per test and a final `SUMMARY: N tests | F failed | C changed since the snapshot`; it exits non-zero unless both are 0.
- **Snapshots** (`src/snap/<test>.txt`, `src/snapcmp.py`): most tests only print numbers, so each has a snapshot of the lines a clean run prints every time (built from three runs; lines that differ between runs are left out). A later run that no longer prints one of them is reported CHANGED with the missing lines. The date, the real-clock weather words and the rotating daily city quest are normalised for every test; numbers are masked as `#` in the comparison for every test except the 33 listed in `src/snap/exact.txt` (steady across many runs), so a True/False flip, changed words or a missing line still fail but timing noise (live duels, animation, positions, pixels) does not; a numeric drift in a masked test is NOT caught, so put a real expectation in a `check()` when a number matters. A test that is not clean is repeated once before it is reported; if the repeat is clean it is shown as FLAKY (never hidden). After an INTENDED change, read the diff, then accept it with `bash src/snap-build.sh name` (one test, three fresh runs) or `bash src/snap-build.sh` (all, about 25 minutes). Never rebuild to make a red run green without reading the diff.
- **What the suite catches, measured by mutation (Nov 2026, Dan: "are these tests still reliable?")**: five deliberate bugs were injected one at a time. Caught: the cold gaining wind (`windtest`), the gym vines never opening (`gymtest`), a changed NPC line (`scaffoldtest`). Missed, then closed with `balancetest.py` (explicit `check()` expectations, in `@smoke`): a pure number drift (DUEL_HP 2.65 -> 4: masked numbers hide it everywhere else) and the seal wording reverting to conquest (nothing asserted on the leaders' lines). Rule of thumb from this: a snapshot proves "still prints what it printed"; anything that MATTERS as a number or a sentence needs a `check()` with the expected value written down. Re-run the mutation idea whenever a new system lands: break it on purpose and see what goes red.
- **Known stale checks** (print a constant False; kept visible, labelled STALE, not hidden): `shinytest` sprite comparison and `towertest` "replay blocked" (it simulates the retired turn-based flow; the live Tower is covered by `acttest5`). The first audit of the snapshots also found and fixed: `evotest` fingerprint broken by the new `seedLabel` field, `jittertest` disturbed by wind (now run with Reduce motion), `leadtest` (the Moss sprouts bed sat in the zone's only big clearing: `addSpot` now picks the LEAST open tile near the hint so zones keep their open ground for fights), `acttest` hook artifact.
- **New tests** use `from chk import check, done` (`src/chk.py`): `check('what must be true', cond, detail)` prints ok / FAIL and `done()` exits non-zero if any failed. Test anything animated over real time (`windrhythmtest.py` logs the pose against the clock): stills hide rhythm bugs.
- `p17`, `menutest`, `transitiontest`, `devtest`, `compattest` run without the seed hook; everything else with it (`run-tests.sh` handles it). `wplay` uses real randomness: repeat a flaky result before calling it a regression. `supatest.py` (real cloud) is separate, see `CLAUDE.md`.
- Where the time goes and what would cut it further (measured Nov 2026): the sum of test times is about 1100 s, 480 s of it explicit sleeps (704 `wait_for_timeout` calls; 152 of them are the 1.0 to 1.5 s wait after `goto` for APP to exist). The next real saving is replacing those post-load sleeps with `wait_for_function("typeof APP!=='undefined' && APP.game")` (about 180 s of sum, done mechanically across 84 files) and shortening the 300 to 900 ms sleeps after actions into waits on a condition; the two real-time wind tests (100 s together) are long by design. Sub-agents do not speed tests up: the limit is this machine's 4 cores and Chromium, and parallelism is a shell matter (`xargs -P`), already done.
- Feel, tempo, video and real phones cannot be judged from here; say so in the report.

## Eight facings (Dan, Nov 2026: "8 directional animations for the trainer and the critters")
- The trainer (`PXW.frames` in pxworld.js): eight facings, three frames each, frame 0 the idle. The quarter views are the same parts re-arranged the way the reference sheets do it, not flips of the front: the front quarter has the face off-centre with both eyes, the back quarter is hair with the edge of a cheek, both show only the near hand. Left facings are the right ones mirrored. NPCs use the same frames but keep four facings (their sight lines are four-way, `inSight`).
- The facing comes from the velocity in eight 45-degree sectors (`dir8` in world.js) for the player and the follower; the attack's direction table has the diagonals (`DIRS` in action.js). `WORLD.facing()` and `WORLD.walkFrame()` are test accessors. `facingtest` (ui, smoke) checks the sectors, the frames and the walk cycle against the clock.
- Critters (`PIX.sprite(k, mode, facing)`, `geometry()` in pixel.js): eight facings from the same geometry, no flips of the front. The back is the parts mirrored, no face, the tail drawn in front of the body and a darker line down the spine; the profile is the body narrowed to 0.8 with one eye and the mouth at the far edge and the tail moved behind; the quarter views are 0.92 wide with the face shifted toward the facing (the back quarters are the back at 0.92 with a sliver of one eye at the far edge, the outer third of it, clipped by the body, ink only). Left facings mirror the right ones in silhouette; the pixel shading stays lit from the top-left on purpose. Tails are found by position (a part centred past 72% of the body's width and below 35% of its height), which covers the stage-1 tails and the evolution kits' tails.
- In play: the lead critter and the one walking behind draw `imgFor(dir)` (per-facing images made on first use, in `APP_FOLLOWER`), wild and duel critters draw `H.img(k, facing)` from their velocity (`dir8` in action.js). ## Type verbs (Dan, Nov 2026: "rework the attacks"; one verb per type, from the place's own metaphor; Frost and Ember first)
- Found: every critter had the same four slots (quick, heavy at 75% to hit, guard, special = a status) and in the live duel every one of them was the same lunge. The type changed a number and a colour, never the verb.
- Built (`action.js`, `VERBS`, `verb` state, `stepVerb`, `drawVerb`): the special slot (wheel slot 4) is the type's verb for all eight types; the old status special survives only in the turn-based path. Powers, the beats table and the turn-based path are untouched.
  - **Frost, Deep Freeze** (stillness): press it and stand still; a ring of ice forms at 0.6 tiles and widens 1 tile per second to 2.6; every 0.5 s what it touches takes 40% of the quick's power, is held 1.1 s (`m.frozen`: no step, no attack, drawn pale) and chilled; the first step (3 px) or 3.2 s shatters it; cooldown 2.2 s after a shatter, 3 s after a full ring. No other attack while it stands.
  - **Ember, Scorch** (the fire that has to be fed): hold the attack button (or J) to feed the flame at the critter's mouth; release a cone 1.3 to 2.9 tiles long, 70 degrees wide, at 50% to 180% of the quick's power by charge (0.12 s to 1.3 s of feeding), burn 3; a tap is a puff. The wheel then opens by dragging off the button (22 px), not by holding; `chargeStart/End/Cancel`, `chargeable` are the entry points.
  - **Tide, Undertow** (what comes back): a wave goes out 2.6 tiles ahead in a lane 1.6 tiles wide; what it touches takes 50% of the quick's power, is soaked (weaken 3) and dragged back to one tile from you (`m.kx/ky` sized to the distance, `m.pull` draws the trail), no further; cooldown 3 s on a hit, 1.4 s on a miss.
  - **Moss, Root Bind** (tending): roots take the nearest critter within 3.2 tiles where it stands: 50% of the quick's power, drained (sap 3), `m.rooted` 2.4 s during which it cannot step (`walk` refuses) but can still attack; while you stay within 1.6 tiles of what the roots hold and are hurt, they feed you 2% of your max HP every 0.5 s; with nothing in reach, roots at your feet and a 1.2 s cooldown.
  - **Volt, Arc Dash** (the wind that passes through): the critter crosses 3 tiles in 0.3 s along its facing (`WORLD.setPos` each frame, stopped by walls, never by critters); everything it passes within 0.95 tile takes 60% of the quick's power, is dazed 2 and pushed sideways; afterimages and a Volt trail; cooldown 2.2 s.
  - **Dusk, Veil** (the dark you step into): `st.veil` 1.6 s, the critter drawn at 0.35 alpha (`ACT.veilAlpha`, read by `drawCritterAt`); every hit against it is lost (`hurtHero` returns early, 'lost'); the first blow it lands while veiled is 1.8x ('From the dark') and ends the veil; 3.5 s cooldown after the veil ends either way.
  - **Stone, Bulwark** (what is kept): a wall on the tile you face (not on a solid tile, a wall or a critter: then a 0.8 s cooldown and nothing); `walls` are solid to critters, charges and shots (`tileFree`, `walk`), a charge or a shot into it costs it 1 of 3 hp, it lasts 7 s; cracks show the hits; cooldown 4.5 s. You can walk through your own wall.
  - **Bloom, Soothing Scent** (gives without being asked): petals around you for 2.4 s, radius 2 tiles, following you; every 0.5 s you and the bench heal 6% of max; every critter in the ring is calmed 1.2 s (`m.calmT`: idle, no step, no attack); cooldown 5 s.
- `verbtest` (battle, smoke) times all eight against the clock: the ring widens monotonically, holds and chills two placed critters, no other attack meanwhile, a step shatters it; the charge rises while held and the fed cone deals at least twice the puff and burns. Trainers are marked declined in the test (a challenge pauses the world).
- **The heavy blow** (slot 2 on the wheel): fed, not rolled. Hold and the blow gathers (the same charge as Ember's flame, `verb.kind === 'heavy'`), let go and it lands at 0.6x when tapped up to 1.5x when fed 1.3 s, lunging a fifth further; it never misses (the 75% roll is skipped for slot 1 in the live duel; the turn-based path keeps it). `swing(mi, mul)` is the lunge, factored out of `attack()`.
- **Guard** (slot 3): a stance. Hold and the guard stays up (`st.stanceOn`, `ACT.stance()`), let go and it drops; it breaks on its own at 2.5 s with a 1.5 s cooldown; a tap under 0.25 s leaves the old short guard (1.3 s) instead, so a tap still works.
- The duel sim's `smart` bot knows the verbs (`duelsim.py`: Frost stands and freezes at close range, Ember feeds 1 s and releases, Tide pulls from 1.8 to 2.5 tiles, Moss roots within 3, Volt dashes within 2.8, Dusk veils when under 70%, Stone walls against shooters and chargers, Bloom petals under 60%; the fed heavy a third of the time in reach, the quick otherwise) and does not walk out of a standing ring. Results (Nov 7, 40 fights each, hero stage 2 Lv 12 vs the leaders and route trainers; stage 3 Lv 18 vs the long nights), mash bot / verb bot, win %:
  - ivy 98 / 95, wren 98 / 75, bram 95 / 53, glacia 50 / 8, kiki 80 / 13, blaze 23 / 0, marlo 40 / 15, coral 3 / 0; night 5: 100 / 98, night 10: 98 / 93, night 15: 98 / 100, night 20: 73 / 75.
  - Reading: the verbs are control, not damage. A bot that never moves or dodges gains nothing from a held, pulled, dazed or calmed foe, and pays every verb's cooldown in lost quick slashes, so the verb bot loses to the mash bot against anything that fights back hard. Against the long nights, where lasting is the goal, the two bots are level. This is a measurement of the bot as much as of the verbs: a person uses a verb to avoid a hit, which the sim cannot value. Tuned on this run: the fed heavy is 2x and gathers in 0.8 s (it was 1.5x in 1.3 s, strictly worse than tapping); verb cooldowns came down (Frost ring 1.5 s after a full ring, 1.2 after a shatter; Ember 0.8 + 0.8 per charge; Tide 2 / 1.2; Moss 2; Volt 1.4; Dusk 2.5; Stone 3; Bloom 3.5); the ring chills at 55% of the quick.
  - The mash numbers are the live-duel baseline the leaders were tuned to earlier (the quick slash never changed); they are not a regression from the verbs. Blaze 23 and Coral 3 at stage 2 Lv 12 say those two want a stronger party than this run gave them.
  - Not done: a bot that moves, so control has a value in the sim; until then the leaders' and the Watch's numbers stand on the mash bot.
